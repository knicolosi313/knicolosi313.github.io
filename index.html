About

Curiosity, turned into systems.

Two minors, one question

I'm studying Software Engineering with minors in Artificial Intelligence and Finance. That looks like hedging until you notice what both fields are actually about: making decisions under uncertainty, at speed, from incomplete data. FinRobot came directly out of that overlap: an agent doing the research legwork that analysts do by hand. The pairing wasn't a backup plan; it was the whole idea.

I build the boring parts properly

Most of what I find interesting isn't the model call; it's everything around it. Market APIs disagree with each other. Language models are confidently wrong. Study material arrives as a messy PDF. The demo is easy; making it hold up on the second run is the actual work, and it's where I spend most of my time.

Leading 525 people

As President of the RIT Artificial Intelligence Club, I run a 525-member organization, and the part I didn't expect to be doing is sales. I cold-pitched technology companies until one said yes, landing the club's first corporate sponsorship, and now coordinate with them to bring in engineers to give technical talks. Turns out explaining why your work matters is a separate skill from doing it.

Multi-agent pipeline

FinRobot

Type a stock ticker, get a formatted research report. Three MCP servers running three different model providers: Perplexity for retrieval, Claude for formatting, Matplotlib for charts because charts shouldn't be a model's job, all coordinated by a Gemini orchestrator. It now refuses to draw a chart it can't get real prices for, which is the fix I'd defend hardest. Built with the RIT AI Club.
